Authority & Strategic Searching

This short module covers two ideas from the ACRL Framework for Information Literacy that show up in almost every research project you'll do at Duke.

Authority Is Constructed and Contextual

"Best source" depends on what you're trying to do. You'll practice matching real research situations to the source that's the strongest authority for that specific case.

Searching as Strategic Exploration

Good searching is iterative — you refine as you go. You'll run a simulated database search and watch how your results change as you sharpen your query.

        Key takeaways

        • Authority is constructed and contextual. There's no universal "best" source — the right authority depends on your specific research need.
        • Searching is strategic exploration. Your first query is a starting point, not a final answer. Expect to revise your keywords, add or drop concepts, and use tools like quotation marks and Boolean operators (AND / OR / NOT) to sharpen your results.

        Need help applying this to a real assignment? Duke University Libraries subject librarians can meet with you one-on-one — look for "Ask a Librarian" on the library website.
